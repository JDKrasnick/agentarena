What this system is actually doing.

Agent Arena is a local evidence pipeline wrapped around competing coding agents. This walkthrough separates the current implementation from a simpler target: two contestants, one semantic judge, one deterministic harness, and a deliberately thin arena coordinator.

Start here if you are losing the plot

The product still has three centers of gravity: run-level coordination, round execution and adjudication, and a human-controlled delivery boundary.

src/core/arena.ts currently connects almost everything. The proposed seam makes it consume complete round replays instead of directing worktrees, agents, tests, and verdicts itself.

The proposed three-agent model is a simplification target, not yet the behavior described by PRODUCT.md and docs/MVP.md.

The big system

A fight begins as user intent and ends as an authenticated patch decision. Between those points, Agent Arena converts uncertain model work into increasingly constrained evidence.

Proposed target: three agents, two kinds of truth

Architecture direction · August 7, 2026

Two contestant agents

Each agent builds its own solution, authors executable attacks against the opponent, and repairs its own patch. A separate case-builder agent is no longer required.

One semantic judge

The verifier independently decides validity, canonical root defect, and severity from the frozen task packet plus execution evidence. It does not operate the harness.

One deterministic round engine

Worktrees, commands, retries, comparison, feedback, and scoring stay inside a round boundary. The arena sees only a complete replay and outcome for each round.

The credibility path

The most important architectural idea is not “two agents.” It is the series of gates between a model saying something is wrong and the system allowing that claim to change the result.

One attack round, as messages

The attacker may author the test, but cannot declare it valid. The harness reports what reproduced; the verifier independently decides what the failure means and how severe it is.

1. Executable claim

The attacker supplies expected behavior, a test-only patch, and a focused command.

2. Mechanical policy

The round engine checks allowed paths, capabilities, clean application, and symmetric inputs.

3. Reproduction

The harness repeats author, target, and required checks and records structured diagnostics without assigning severity.

4. Semantic judgment

The verifier independently decides validity, canonical root defect, and severity from the RunSpec and evidence.

5. Replayable scoring

A published confidence rule combines severity and reproduction state; the round engine records the event.

Verifier severity

Low 5 · Medium 15 · High 30 · Critical 50. The judge selects this independently.

×
Harness confidence

Reproduced: 1.0×. Ambiguous or infrastructure failure: 0× and retain diagnostics.

=
Scored damage

One deterministic event per canonical root defect, applied simultaneously after the round closes.

Recommended multiplier policy: treat harness confirmation as the full-value gate, not a bonus above 1.0×. A 1.1× verification bonus would mix confidence with impact and double-count evidence quality. If semantic-only attacks must score, make them explicitly discounted, such as 0.5×, rather than inflating reproduced attacks.

Trust and authority boundaries

The system intentionally separates who may suggest, who may execute, who may decide what counts, and who may change the user’s repository or GitHub state.

A hard look

The architecture has a coherent safety story. The questions below are about whether the implementation shape will stay understandable and reliable as the product grows.

What is structurally strong

  • A compact immutable RunSpec can preserve fair inputs and replayability without requiring a fully normalized semantic contract.
  • Contestant identity is separated from provider identity, which supports mirror matches and provenance-safe artifacts.
  • Attacker authorship, deterministic reproduction, semantic judgment, and scoring remain separate authority boundaries.
  • Health is replayable from a ledger; scoring is not hidden inside model judgment.
  • The verifier can own severity without owning execution, keeping impact judgment independent from harness confidence.
  • Unix timeouts now terminate the process tree, so a descendant cannot silently exceed the configured budget.
  • Human acceptance and external delivery are distinct, digest-bound authorization boundaries.
  • Infrastructure uncertainty is modeled explicitly instead of being silently charged to contestants.

Where pressure is accumulating

  • Arena is the gravity well. At 2,512 lines, it owns lifecycle, worktrees, prompts, review collection, validation ordering, repair, recovery, reporting, and recommendation coordination.
  • The domain schema is monolithic. Persisted types, operational types, review, delivery, and migration concerns share one 1,156-line file.
  • Policy and orchestration are close together. “What should happen” and “how this run invokes it” are often expressed in the same methods.
  • Symmetric lanes are inconsistently concurrent. Implementations run in parallel; several review, attack, validation, and repair loops are sequential.
  • One verifier becomes a semantic trust point. Structured outputs, anonymization, conservative ambiguity handling, and saved rationales matter more when auxiliary judge roles are removed.
  • No local harness perfectly replicates production. The product should report bounded reproduction confidence rather than modeling every unobserved environment difference as something an agent can repair.
  • Evidence overlay is a high-authority Git operation. Its remove, base-restore, and apply ordering now protects the oracle, so path parsing and policy validation are correctness-critical.
  • Deadline enforcement is intentionally abrupt. Group SIGKILL closes the budget loophole but gives processes no graceful cleanup window.
  • Local execution remains a trust boundary. Worktrees isolate changes, not hostile code or authority already available to the OS account.

Questions worth forcing now

  • Can Arena be reduced to start, advance, finalize, and consume RoundReplay?
  • Which stage can be rerun safely after interruption, and which artifacts prove idempotence?
  • What is the smallest typed interface between RoundEngine and Arena?
  • Should ambiguous semantic-only attacks be report-only, or receive an explicitly discounted confidence multiplier?
  • Can evidence-path precedence be represented as a typed validation plan before any destructive worktree operation begins?
  • Does the “champion vs recommendation” distinction clarify the product, or create two competing truths for users?
  • Which invariants are enforced in code, which only in prompts, and which only in documentation?
  • What evidence will prove that three rounds outperform one round enough to justify the cost?

The next architectural decisions

These are not automatic refactors. They are seams to decide deliberately before feature growth makes the current shape expensive to change.

  1. Extract one round engine first. Give it a frozen RunSpec plus current patches and require one immutable RoundReplay in return. Do not split every phase into a new service.
  2. Reduce to three model roles. Contestants A and B build, attack, and repair; one anonymized verifier returns validity, canonical root, severity, and rationale.
  3. Publish the confidence rule. Keep verifier severity as base damage. Use reproduced 1.0× and ambiguous 0× for the MVP; reserve discounted semantic-only scoring for a later experiment.
  4. Replace harness accommodation agents with failure policy. Retry once in fresh worktrees, attach structured diagnostics, then withdraw the attack or mark the run inconclusive.
  5. Separate domain schemas from persisted version schemas. Make RunSpec, RoundReplay, verifier verdict, and storage migrations explicit boundaries.
  6. Instrument the product hypothesis. Persist enough token, cost, time, and outcome data to compare first-passing, one-round, and three-round strategies.
